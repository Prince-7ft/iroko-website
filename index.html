History of Iroko

Iroko is one of the oldest towns and a fortified town, located about nineteen miles from Ibadan on the road to Oyo, and thirteen miles from Oyo. It is one of the earliest settlements before Iwo (IJAIYE, IKEREKU) and nearby towns. It was a royal town, which made it larger and more prominent than these other towns. The founder of early Iroko was Oyenniya from the Egbado region; he came from the town of Ake to settle here, and this is why his descendants are still considered Ake people today. When the people of Ake dispersed to settle in places where they could farm and live freely, many of them went to Abeokuta, Ijebu, and other areas, leaving only Oyenniya and his family in Ake. Oyenniya called his people together and told them that since all other Ake people had left, he would move from Ake to wherever he found suitable to settle, and that he wanted all his relatives to come with him. The people agreed, but when the day came to leave, many did not follow. Seeing this, Oyenniya, together with his wife and children, left Ake alone. They traveled with their children and spouse, but the journey was difficult, and they did not reach their destination immediately. This is why Oyenniya named the place he eventually settled Ero-ko-ko, which later became Iroko, and the name has remained to the present day. After some years, the people of the town recognized Oyenniya as their leader and called him Ekun Iroko (or the Iroko ruler). He gave land to people and acted as the town’s head.
